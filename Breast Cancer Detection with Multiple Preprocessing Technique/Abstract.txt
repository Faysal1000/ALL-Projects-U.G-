Breast cancer classification from histopathological images is challenged by staining variability, tissue artifacts, and the high computational cost of deep learning models, which restricts deployment in resource-constrained clinical environments. This paper proposes a novel lightweight convolutional neural network for binary breast cancer classification using the BreaKHis dataset. The proposed architecture integrates Ghost modules for computational efficiency, squeeze-and-excitation and coordinate attention mechanisms for enhanced channel–spatial feature representation, and residual learning for stable optimization, resulting in only 1.72 million trainable parameters. Using this model, we systematically investigate the impact of five preprocessing strategies—Macenko, Vahadane, Reinhard, Ruifrok stain normalization, and Otsu-based tissue separation—across multiple magnification levels. Experimental results demonstrate that Otsu-based tissue separation at 400× magnification consistently outperforms stain normalization approaches, achieving a test AUC of 98.54% and accuracy of 95.33% with a 95% confidence interval of 92.62–97.67%. Model interpretability is further validated using Grad-CAM++ and Score-CAM, which highlight pathology-relevant and spatially consistent regions. The proposed framework achieves competitive performance with significantly fewer parameters than existing methods, making it well suited for accurate, explainable, and efficient breast cancer classification on edge and low-resource devices.
